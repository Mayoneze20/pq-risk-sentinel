# Threat model — PQ Risk Sentinel

## Assets

- The vault's testnet QOR balance (10 QOR at deployment; 5 QOR after the
  demonstrated exit).
- The integrity of the decision pipeline: a decision that executes must be
  the model's decision, signed by the agent, inside its guardrails.

## Adversaries and what stops them

### 1. Thief (or quantum attacker) with the agent's classical key — the headline case
The agent's secp256k1 private key is **published** (`docs/BREAK_GLASS.md`),
modelling total classical compromise.
- *Vault decisions:* `executeDecision` requires an ML-DSA-87 signature
  verified by precompile 0x0a01 against the registered key (0x0a02). A
  classical ECDSA signature over the exact decision bytes reverts with
  `BadPqcSignature`. **Demonstrated on chain** (EVIDENCE.md).
- *Owner surface:* the agent is not the owner; `onlyOwner` reverts.
  **Demonstrated on chain.**
- *Native lane:* the agent's own transactions are admitted only inside a
  hybrid-signed authorisation window under a chain-enforced spending rule.
  Opening a window is itself a hybrid-signed message — the classical key
  alone cannot do it.
- *Residual:* the attacker can spend the agent account's own small QOR
  balance on fees within an already-open window. Accepted: the balance is
  operational pocket change on a testnet; the vault is unreachable.

### 2. Malicious or mistaken relayer
Anyone may relay a decision. The signed message binds chain ID, vault
address, action, confidence, features hash, nonce and deadline block:
- a signature replays nowhere (nonce consumed by every verified decision,
  executed or ignored);
- a signature for one vault/action/confidence is invalid for any other
  (tampering reverts — covered by tests);
- an expired decision reverts (`DecisionExpired`).

### 3. The model itself (bad or manipulated scoring)
The contract does not trust the model's judgement, only its identity:
- confidence floor: low-conviction decisions are ignored on the record;
- shared cooldown over protective actions: even a compromised model cannot
  machine-gun pauses/exits;
- exit cap: one decision moves at most 50% of the vault, and only to the
  fixed safe address — the model cannot choose a destination;
- hold cannot move funds at all.

### 4. Compromised agent PQC key (out of scope of the demo, mitigated by design)
If the ML-DSA-87 secret itself leaked, an attacker could sign decisions —
but still only within the guardrails above (caps, cooldown, fixed safe
address), and the owner can `setPaused`, rotate the agent's on-chain key
(the vault reads the registry every call), or `emergencyWithdraw`. Key
custody for the demo: the key exists only inside the agent's signer process
and the throwaway testnet key files, which live outside this repository.

### 5. Owner key compromise
The owner can retune guardrails and drain via `emergencyWithdraw`. This is
deliberate — the human is the final override — and it is the standard
single-key caveat of a hackathon build. A production version would put the
owner behind a multisig and the safe address behind a timelock.

## Known limitations

- The market feed in this build is the fest demo market (synthetic windows
  generated by `model/model_service.py` scenarios); the scoring core is
  feed-agnostic and was originally written against live DEX swap events.
- ML-DSA verification cost makes `executeDecision` gas-heavy (1.5M gas
  budget); fine on QoreChain's fee market, worth noting for other chains.
- The vault's staleness model is a signed deadline block, not a wall-clock
  age: a decision is fresh if it lands before its deadline, however old it
  is in seconds. Block-time manipulation on a testnet is out of scope.
